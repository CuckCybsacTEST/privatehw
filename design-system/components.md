# Components

These are the component-level rules that keep the product visually consistent.

## Hero premium

- The hero should feel like an invite into a private room.
- Use one strong headline, one supporting line, and one dominant CTA.
- Add atmosphere with gradients, soft glow, or smoke-like overlays, but preserve legibility.
- The home hero should imply access and exclusivity rather than show everything at once.

## CTA principal

- Primary CTA: filled premium red with high contrast text.
- Secondary CTA: smoke-dark outline or low-emphasis fill.
- Tertiary CTA: ghost treatment only when the action is minor.
- Button labels should be short, clear, and conversion-oriented.
- Buttons must keep a minimum touch target of 44px.

## Buttons

- Buttons should feel premium rather than playful.
- Hover should be a warm lift, not a flashy effect.
- Destructive actions must stay visually separated.

## Inputs

- Inputs should use a dark surface, clear border, and visible focus ring.
- Labels must be placed above or directly associated with the field.
- Helper text should sit under the field and remain readable on mobile.
- Error state must show border, message, and if needed an icon.
- Avoid overly decorative input chrome.
- Private or contact-oriented forms should feel safe, calm, and high trust.

## Private contact form

- Keep the form visually quiet and premium.
- Reduce unnecessary fields when the goal is private contact or inquiry.
- Make consent, privacy, and validation states obvious.
- Use one dominant submit action and one optional cancellation path.

## Cards

- Cards should group one meaningful unit of content.
- Use a clear header, supporting body, and one primary action when needed.
- Maintain internal rhythm with consistent padding.
- Preview cards should hint at the content while preserving privacy and intrigue.
- Membership and pricing cards should feel exclusive, not promotional-brochure cheap.
- Avoid mixing too many visual treatments in one card family.

## Cards de preview

- Use layered surfaces, subtle borders, and controlled glow.
- Show enough information to create desire, not enough to overwhelm the user.
- Keep captions concise and avoid explicit detail in supporting labels.

## Gallery

- Galleries should feel curated and cinematic.
- Use strong framing, consistent aspect ratios, and simple navigation.
- On mobile, move to a single-column or swipeable pattern.
- If content is protected, show a teaser state rather than the full set.

## Tables

- Tables are for comparison and operational density.
- Headers should be visually stronger than body rows.
- Row hover should be subtle and functional.
- Use numeric alignment and status badges where they improve scanability.
- On narrow screens, move to stacked cards or an alternative layout.

## Modals

- Use for confirmations, focused edits, and short forms.
- Keep a clear title and close control.
- Do not force wide, content-heavy dialogs on mobile.
- Put the primary action last in the visual flow.

## Modal +18

- The +18 modal must be accessible by keyboard and easy to dismiss.
- It should feel firm, premium, and privacy-first.
- Keep copy short and neutral.
- Use clear acceptance and decline actions.
- Do not over-animate the modal or use aggressive imagery.

## Navigation

- Desktop navigation may be horizontal, sidebar, or hybrid depending on the page.
- Mobile navigation should stay compact and thumb-friendly.
- Active state must be obvious without relying on color alone.
- Avoid unnecessary navigation depth.
- Navbar should feel like a velvet rope, not a utility bar.
- Footer should support trust, navigation, and compliance without visual noise.

## Pricing / membership cards

- Use strong hierarchy for plan names, value, and savings.
- Highlight the recommended tier with subtle glow and stronger border.
- Keep comparison information concise.
- Avoid aggressive discount styling or bargain-store visuals.

## Exclusive content section

- Use tease-first composition: a premium preview, a small amount of metadata, and a clear access path.
- Keep restricted content protected behind a confirmation or sign-in flow.
- Make exclusivity feel intentional rather than punitive.

## Badges and chips

- Use for status, categorization, or compact metadata.
- Keep labels short.
- Use semantic colors only when the badge conveys meaning.

## Loading states

- Use skeletons for content areas.
- Use spinner or inline progress only for short actions.
- Preserve layout so the screen does not jump.

## Empty states

- Explain the empty condition.
- Suggest one next step if possible.
- Keep the tone calm and operational.
- Avoid generic placeholder art unless it improves comprehension.

## Content surfaces

- Prioritize readability over decorative treatment.
- Media, product, and revenue surfaces should feel premium and organized.
- Keep metadata structured and consistent.
- Avoid explicit surface copy when a neutral placeholder or tease is sufficient.

## Anti-patterns

- Avoid default UI-kit spacing that makes every surface feel the same.
- Avoid over-reliance on borders without depth.
- Avoid dense icon clusters with no labels.
- Avoid motion that does not communicate state or intent.
- Avoid casino cues, harsh neon overload, and cheap nightclub cliches.
